Introduction to SIP

What is an SIP & How Does It Create Wealth?

Invest a fixed amount regularly according to your long-term financial goals and let compounding work for you.

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows individuals to invest manageable sums periodically (monthly or quarterly) into diversified equity and hybrid mutual funds. By investing consistently through market highs and lows, SIP leverages Rupee Cost Averaging, eliminating the need to time the market.

Disciplined Habit
Automates monthly savings before discretionary spending.
Rupee Cost Averaging
Buys more units when markets dip, lowering average cost.
Power of Compounding
Returns earn returns over decades, scaling modest sums into substantial wealth.
Complete Flexibility
Start small from ₹500 or ₹1,000/mo. Increase, pause, or stop anytime without penalties.

What is an SIP?

An SIP is a way of investing into a mutual fund scheme where a fixed amount is invested periodically instead of a single lump sum. The investor decides four things up front:

  • The mutual fund scheme to invest into
  • The investment amount per instalment
  • The investment duration of the SIP
  • The SIP date on which the instalment is processed

On each SIP date the amount is debited from the registered bank account and units are allotted at the applicable NAV of that date. Because the NAV changes, the number of units bought changes with every instalment.

How does an SIP work?

The operational flow is fully automated through an Electronic Clearing Service (ECS) or National Automated Clearing House (NACH) mandate:

1Select Fund & DateChoose scheme, amount and preferred debit date.
2Auto-DebitBank debits the instalment automatically.
3AllotmentUnits credited to folio at current day's NAV.
4AccumulateUnits grow with compounding over time.

Because this runs automatically, you never need to remember dates, write cheques or watch daily market movements to make your contribution.

Compounding Principle

Start Early: Mr. Early vs Mr. Late

Compounding delivers its largest gains in the final years of an investment, which means starting ten years sooner creates a compounding advantage that higher instalments later cannot easily overcome.

Mr. Early starts at age 25, invests for 10 years, and stops. Mr. Late waits until age 35, invests the same monthly amount for 25 years. Despite Mr. Late investing 2.5× more total money, Mr. Early's head-start gives his capital ten extra years of pure compounding.

Market Volatility Defense

Rupee Cost Averaging: How Falls Work for You

When the NAV falls, your fixed monthly instalment purchases more units; when the NAV rises, it purchases fewer units. Over market cycles, your average cost per unit stays lower than the average NAV over the same period.

Month Monthly SIP Applicable NAV Units Allotted Market Behaviour
Month 1 ₹5,000 ₹50.00 100.00 Normal Market
Month 2 ₹5,000 ₹40.00 125.00 Market Dip (Buys 25% more units)
Month 3 ₹5,000 ₹33.33 150.00 Deep Correction (Buys 50% more units)
Month 4 ₹5,000 ₹50.00 100.00 Market Recovers to Starting Point
Total / Summary ₹20,000 Avg NAV: ₹43.33 475.00 Units Avg Cost / Unit: ₹42.11
Portfolio Value at Month 4:
475 units × ₹50 = ₹23,750 (+18.75%)
The NAV ended exactly where it started (₹50), yet the portfolio earned an 18.75% gain because of units bought cheaply during the dip.
What a Lump Sum Would Have Done:
₹20,000 @ ₹50 = 400 units = ₹20,000 (0.00%)
A lump sum invested in Month 1 would have returned zero over the four months, while SIP turned the dip into a profit engine.
Investor Psychology

Market Volatility: Why Staying Invested Matters

The single biggest reason retail investors underperform mutual fund returns is emotional stopping during market corrections. When markets fall, three behavioural traps occur:

Stopping the SIP

Stopping during a market dip forfeits the chance to accumulate units at discount valuations, ruining the mathematical benefit of rupee cost averaging.

Redeeming in Panic

Converting temporary paper losses into permanent realized losses. Historically, market corrections have been followed by multi-year compounding cycles.

Waiting for the "Bottom"

Market bottoms cannot be predicted consistently. Investors who exit typically re-enter after markets have already rallied significantly, missing the recovery.

Asset Allocation

Investment Horizon & Fund Category Suitability

SIPs should always be matched with an appropriate time horizon. Equity funds need time to navigate business cycles and short-term volatility:

5+ Years Horizon
Equity Mutual Funds
Large-Cap, Flexi-Cap, Mid-Cap, Small-Cap funds.

Suitable for retirement, child higher education and wealth creation goals. Short-term volatility is high, but 5-to-10 year rolling returns have historically compensated for market risk.

3 to 5 Years Horizon
Hybrid / Balanced Funds
Balanced Advantage, Multi-Asset, Aggressive Hybrid.

Blends equity growth with debt stability. Dynamic asset allocation manages downside during market falls while participating in market upside.

1 to 3 Years Horizon
Debt / Liquid Funds
Short Duration, Corporate Bond, Liquid, Arbitrage.

Focuses on capital preservation and liquidity. Suitable for short-term goals, emergency buffers or parking funds before deploying into equity via STP.

Clarity & Compliance

Practical SIP Rules & Frequently Asked Questions

Yes. SIP is completely flexible. Unlike recurring bank deposits or insurance policies, there are no penalties for pausing or stopping your SIP. You can pause it for 1–6 months or discontinue it completely. Your accumulated units continue to stay invested and grow.

A Step-Up SIP automatically increases your monthly investment by a predetermined percentage (e.g. 10% per year) or a fixed rupee amount as your salary or business income rises each year. Stepping up your SIP dramatically accelerates wealth accumulation and matches your savings with salary increments.

Exit loads depend on the specific mutual fund scheme. Most equity schemes charge a 1% exit load if units are redeemed within 365 days of their individual purchase date. Because each SIP instalment is treated as a separate purchase, units older than 1 year generally carry 0% exit load. Always check scheme documents.

Under current tax laws for equity-oriented funds:
  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): Units held for 12 months or less are taxed at 20% plus applicable surcharge and cess.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): Units held for more than 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh in a financial year (gains up to ₹1.25 Lakh per year are tax-exempt).
Tax rules are subject to change. Please consult a qualified tax advisor for your personal situation.
